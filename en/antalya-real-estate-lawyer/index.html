Real Estate Attorney in Antalya

Real Estate and Tenancy Lawyer in Antalya

Eviction, rent determination, title deed actions and zoning disputes.

Real estate and tenancy law governs rights over immovable property and the resolution of disputes concerning them. The Turkish Civil Code, the Turkish Code of Obligations, the Land Registry Law, the Zoning Law and the Condominium Law form the core legislation.

Property and Tenancy Law in Turkey

The field covers title deed cancellation and registration, partition of co-owned property, pre-emption rights, prevention of interference, mortgages and lease agreements. In a city such as Antalya, where property transactions are frequent, these disputes arise regularly.

Tenancy law governs the relationship between landlord and tenant in residential and roofed commercial premises. Eviction actions, rent determination actions and adaptation actions are the principal proceedings in this area.

Rent increases may not exceed the twelve-month average of the consumer price index announced for the month in which the agreement is renewed. Grounds for eviction, and the notice periods attached to each, are set out exhaustively in the legislation.

What a Real Estate Lawyer Handles

1

Eviction Actions

Proceedings based on the landlord's own need, default in payment or reconstruction of the premises.

2

Rent Determination and Adaptation

Actions to have the rent redetermined in line with current market conditions.

3

Title Deed Cancellation and Registration

Cancellation of entries made contrary to law and registration in the name of the rightful owner.

4

Partition of Co-owned Property

Division of jointly owned property, where necessary through judicial sale.

5

Construction in Return for Land Share

Preparation of agreements between landowner and contractor and resolution of resulting disputes.

6

Condominium Matters

Common charges, use of shared areas and annulment of decisions of the owners' assembly.

In Summary

Property is for most people the most significant asset they hold. Examining the title register, the zoning status and the terms of the contract before signing prevents most later disputes.
